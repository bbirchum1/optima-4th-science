# Teacher Grading Guide: Optima 4th Grade Science

Source reviewed: every HTML lesson file in this course folder, in filename lesson order.

Teacher note: Generic Canvas submission directions are omitted. For drawings, charts, models, videos, projects, and student-data tasks, grade using the listed look-fors rather than expecting one exact response. Several generated lesson files contain a repeated visible food-chain/energy-in-a-hawk paragraph in Part C even when the checklist names a lesson-specific product. In those cases, this guide treats the checklist product as the intended gradable Part C and gives criteria for it.

## Lesson 1.01 - Draw a Scientist
Filename: lesson-1-01-draw-a-scientist.html

Part A - The Five Rules Check:
- A: accurate
- B: big
- C: color
- D: details
- E: explanations/labels

Part B - Short Answer:
- 5. A label is a word or short phrase that identifies one part of a drawing. A diagram is the whole drawing arranged to communicate information, often with labels, details, and explanations.
- 6. Scientists sketch because drawings record shapes, positions, details, and observations that words alone may miss. Strong answers mention accuracy, evidence, labels, and repeatable records.
- 7. For a butterfly sketch, look for wing shape, antennae, body sections, legs, patterns/colors, symmetry, and labels. Credit details that are specific and observable.

CER Response - What makes someone a scientist?:
- Strong response: A scientist is someone who asks questions and studies the natural world using observations and evidence. In my drawing, I showed a person observing or testing something because scientists collect details before they explain. This proves my claim because science is about careful investigation, not about looking a certain way.

Part C - Draw a Scientist:
- Answers vary. Look for a titled drawing, at least one person actively doing science, a setting or investigation, clear details, and optional labels/tools that support the student's idea. Do not grade artistic skill over scientific detail.

Reflection:
- Answers vary. Look for a comparison between the first sketch and final drawing, plus one specific change in how the student thinks about scientists.

Essential Question Response:
- Strong answer should say scientists can look like anyone and record/share discoveries through observations, scientific sketches, labels, diagrams, evidence, and accuracy.

## Lesson 1.02 - What is a Scientist?
Filename: lesson-1-02-what-is-a-scientist.html

Video Discussion Assignment:
- Part 1 expected content: A scientist is a person who asks questions about the natural world, makes observations, forms hypotheses, conducts experiments or investigations, and uses evidence/scientific method to find answers. Look for at least two vocabulary words used correctly.
- Part 2 answers vary. Look for a clearly named science interest and a reason or question the student wants to investigate.
- Video quality: Student can be seen/heard well enough to understand the response. Do not over-penalize normal student hesitations.

Preparation Notes:
- Answers vary. Look for brief notes defining scientist, naming an area of curiosity, and listing one investigable question.

CER Response - Most important thing a scientist does:
- Accept several claims if evidence is reasonable. Strong answer might claim that observing carefully, testing a hypothesis, or collecting evidence is most important. Evidence should name a step in the scientific method, and reasoning should explain why that step helps scientists answer questions reliably.

Essential Question Response:
- Strong answer: All scientists share curiosity, careful observation, use of evidence, and a process for testing ideas, no matter what they study.

## Lesson 2.01 - Meet Our Earth
Filename: lesson-2-01-meet-our-earth.html

Part A - Earth's Rotation Diagram:
- Answers vary. Look for Earth drawn with an axis from pole to pole, North Pole and South Pole labeled, curved spin arrows, and the note 1 rotation = 24 hours / 1 day.

Part B - Earth's Revolution Diagram:
- Answers vary. Look for Sun in the center, circular/oval orbit, Earth shown in four seasonal positions, Spring/Summer/Fall/Winter labels, direction arrows, consistent tilted axis, and the note 1 revolution = 1 year / 365 days.

Part C - Short Answer:
- 3. Rotation is Earth spinning on its axis and causes day/night. Revolution is Earth moving around the Sun and causes the year/seasons with Earth's tilt.
- 4. Seasons happen because Earth revolves around the Sun while its axis stays tilted. Different hemispheres receive more direct sunlight at different times of year.
- 5. A low noon Sun suggests the sailor is far from the equator or in a season/hemisphere with lower sun angle; early explorers used repeated Sun position, shadows, stars, and Earth patterns to estimate direction and location. Credit careful reasoning, not exact latitude.

CER Response - Why do we have day and night?:
- Strong answer: Day and night happen because Earth rotates. The rotation diagram shows one side of Earth facing the Sun while the other side faces away. As Earth spins, places move into sunlight for day and away from sunlight for night.

Reflection:
- Answers vary. Look for a specific comparison between warm-up sketch and final rotation/revolution diagrams.

Essential Question Response:
- Strong answer should explain that Earth feels still because we move with it, but it rotates on its axis and revolves in orbit around the Sun. Use vocabulary such as rotate, revolve, axis, orbit, hemisphere, or navigate.

## Lesson 5.02 - Weathering and Erosion: Earth's Slow Sculptors
Filename: lesson-5-02-weathering-erosion.html

Part 1 - Vocabulary Match:
- Weathering: D, the breaking of rocks into smaller pieces
- Erosion: B, the movement of broken rock or soil
- Glacier: A, a slow-moving sheet of ice that grinds rocks
- Soil: C, dirt made from tiny rock pieces and organic matter

Part 2 - Cause and Effect Chart:
- Water: breaks, carries, and deposits sediment; can carve valleys/canyons.
- Wind: wears away and moves sand/soil.
- Ice/glaciers: freeze in cracks, grind rock, and carry sediment.
- Plants: roots can crack rocks; plants can also help hold soil in place.

Part 3 - Real-Life Examples:
- Answers vary. Look for a specific place, description of what was seen, and an identified force such as water, wind, ice, roots, or gravity.

CER Response - Water as powerful erosion force:
- Strong answer: Water is powerful because it can move sediment, carve canyons, and change rock over long periods. Evidence may include rivers shaping canyons, waves wearing coastlines, or rain moving soil.

Reflection:
- Answers vary. Look for comparison to the first sketch and one specific surprise or correction.

Essential Question Response:
- Strong answer should explain that water can weather and erode solid rock slowly over millions of years, moving sediment and forming landforms like canyons.

## Lesson 6.02 - Physical Properties of Minerals
Filename: lesson-6-02-minerals.html

Part A - Label the Properties:
- 1. luster
- 2. hardness
- 3. streak
- 4. cleavage

Part B - Short Answer:
- 5. A mineral is a natural solid material with its own properties; a rock is made of one or more minerals.
- 6. Scientists use more than one property because color alone can mislead; many minerals share a color, and one mineral can appear in different colors. Hardness, streak, luster, and cleavage improve accuracy.
- 7. Marble forms when limestone is changed by heat and pressure. The calcite minerals recrystallize/rearrange into a metamorphic rock.

Part C - Mineral Identification Table & Draw:
- Answers vary by chosen mineral. Look for mineral name, reasonable properties (hardness, color, luster, cleavage, streak), rock formation connection, and labeled drawing.

Evidence-Based Response - Mohs Scale:
- Strong answer: Diamond is the hardest mineral on the Mohs scale at 10. Hardness matters because harder minerals resist scratching and can be used for cutting, tools, or durable surfaces.

Essential Question Response:
- Strong answer should explain that minerals can be identified by hardness, luster, cleavage, streak, color, and Mohs scale, and that minerals combine to form rocks.

Part A · Speed & Energy Data Analysis

Q1

Which car was fastest.

Sample answer
Car B was fastest. In 4 seconds it traveled 240 cm — farther than Car A in the same time. Car C only went 140 cm in 3 seconds, which is the slowest. More distance in the same time = faster.
ScoringProficient: names Car B + uses distance/time reasoning.
Q2

Same mass, different distance.

Sample answer
Even though Car A and Car C have the same mass, Car A went farther (200 cm vs 140 cm). That means Car A had MORE energy when it left the ramp — probably because it was moving faster at the bottom of the ramp.
ScoringProficient: links speed/energy. Advanced: notes that energy = speed AND mass together.
Q3

Heaviest always wins?

Sample answer
The data partly supports the friend — Car B (the heaviest at 150g) DID go the farthest. But Car C (same mass as Car A) went the SHORTEST distance, so weight alone doesn't decide. Speed also matters.
ScoringProficient: uses the data to qualify the friend's claim, not just accept or reject it.
Q4

Block energy.

Sample answer
The block got its energy from Car A. The transfer happened during the COLLISION — when Car A hit the block, some of Car A's motion energy moved into the block, making it slide 10 cm.
ScoringProficient: names "collision" and "transfer" correctly.
Q5

Toaster extras.

Sample answer
The table shows the toaster also makes a little LIGHT (when the heating coils glow red). This matters because it means no device converts 100% of input into the main output — some energy always goes somewhere else, usually as light, sound, or extra heat.
ScoringProficient: names the "extra" + explains it matters.
Q6

Conversion sentence.

Examples
Electrical → Flashlight → Light energy + heat. OR: Motion (wind) → Wind turbine → Electrical energy.
ScoringMust include input, device, and output in correct format.
Q7

Metal spoon in hot soup.

Sample answer
The handle feels hot because METAL is a CONDUCTOR. Heat from the soup transfers from one end of the spoon to the other — passing through the metal until the handle is also warm. Heat always moves from hot to cool.
ScoringProficient: uses "conductor" AND "heat transfer."
Q8

Why oven mitt works.

Sample answer
The oven mitt works because it is an INSULATOR. Cloth doesn't let heat pass through easily, so your hand stays cool even when you grab a hot pan.
ScoringProficient: uses "insulator" correctly + explains blocking heat.
CER · Claim
Sample
Yes — faster objects carry more energy, and that's why they can do more when they collide or move.
CER · Evidence
Sample
Evidence 1: In the ramp data, Car A (faster) traveled 200 cm — Car C (slower) only made 140 cm. Evidence 2: A faster-moving Car A pushed a block 10 cm in a collision — a barely-moving car would push a block much less.
CER · Reasoning
Sample
Both pieces of evidence show that the more SPEED an object has, the more energy it carries — enough to move farther or push other things farther. That's why falling rocks, fast cars, and big ocean waves can do so much.
Q9 · Reflection
Open-ended
Accept any honest reflection showing a real shift. Example: "I used to think a heavier object always wins. The data showed speed matters too."

Common misconceptions to watch for:
  • Thinking heavier = always more energy. Speed counts too.
  • Assuming devices convert 100% of input to one output. Some always goes elsewhere (extra heat, sound, light).
  • Confusing conductor with insulator — give a quick "hot soup spoon" demo.
